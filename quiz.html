Question 1 · Logical Reasoning · Necessary Assumption

Stimulus. Kim argues that SKIMS is a stronger brand than its largest competitor. She says: "Our quarterly revenue is higher, and revenue is the truest measure of brand strength."

Which of the following is an assumption Kim's argument requires?

  1. SKIMS spends less on marketing than its competitor.
  2. The competitor's quarterly revenue is publicly disclosed.
  3. No measure other than revenue is a more accurate indicator of brand strength.
  4. SKIMS will continue to outperform the competitor next quarter.
  5. The competitor sells the same product categories as SKIMS.
Answer: C. Kim's premise is that revenue is the truest measure of brand strength. For that to support her conclusion, no better measure can exist. Negate C ("something else is a more accurate measure") and the argument collapses. That's the negation test working.
Question 2 · Logical Reasoning · Strengthen

Stimulus. Cynthia argues that the show should add an episode set entirely in Anguilla, because the last time the cast filmed abroad, ratings climbed sharply during that episode and the one after.

Which of the following, if true, most strengthens Cynthia's argument?

  1. Episodes set abroad cost more to film than domestic episodes.
  2. Internal Bravo research shows audience interest in cast travel episodes has grown each year for the past three seasons.
  3. Anguilla is a popular vacation destination for fans of the show.
  4. The cast has not filmed abroad in two years.
  5. Cynthia would personally enjoy filming in Anguilla.
Answer: B. A strengthener adds evidence that the pattern Cynthia observed will hold. (B) shows a sustained, independent trend toward travel episodes. (C) is about Anguilla specifically, but for fans — not viewers' on-air preferences. (E) is irrelevant to the audience-facing claim.
Question 3 · Logical Reasoning · Weaken

Stimulus. Khloé argues that the Good American denim line is selling well because the recent influencer campaign worked. She points to the fact that the line sold out within 48 hours of launch.

Which of the following, if true, most undermines Khloé's argument?

  1. The campaign featured influencers with large followings.
  2. Other Good American launches have also sold out quickly.
  3. Most fashion launches are restocked within two weeks.
  4. The launch quantity was deliberately limited to one-third of typical launches.
  5. Influencer marketing campaigns are widely used in the industry.
Answer: D. Khloé draws a causal line from campaign → sellout. (D) supplies an alternate explanation: the line sold out because supply was a third of the usual. The campaign isn't being attacked directly — the causal link is being severed from underneath. Classic weakener move.
Question 4 · Logical Reasoning · Flaw

Stimulus. Drew argues: "Kandi has been on this show longer than I have. So whatever she says about how to handle a group trip must be right."

The reasoning in Drew's argument is most vulnerable to criticism on the grounds that it...

  1. Mistakes a sufficient condition for a necessary one.
  2. Generalizes from a single instance to a universal claim.
  3. Treats tenure as proof of being correct.
  4. Confuses correlation with causation.
  5. Relies on circular reasoning.
Answer: C. Drew is committing an appeal-to-authority flaw — using Kandi's seniority as evidence that her view is correct on a matter where seniority doesn't confer expertise. (A), (B), (D), and (E) are real flaw types but don't describe this argument. On flaw questions, name the move first, then find the answer that describes that move.
Question 5 · Logical Reasoning · Parallel Reasoning

Stimulus. If the reunion is filmed in New York, Andy will attend. If Andy attends, Kandi will speak first. So if the reunion is filmed in New York, Kandi will speak first.

Which of the following arguments has the same logical structure as the argument above?

  1. If it rains, the picnic is canceled. The picnic is canceled, so it must have rained.
  2. If the deal closes, the team gets bonuses. The team is getting bonuses. So the deal closed.
  3. If a car is electric, it has a battery. If a car has a battery, it can be plugged in. So if a car is electric, it can be plugged in.
  4. Either the show is renewed or the cast disbands. The show is renewed. So the cast does not disband.
  5. All housewives are dramatic. Marlo is a housewife. So Marlo is dramatic.
Answer: C. The stimulus is a hypothetical syllogism: A→B, B→C, therefore A→C. Only (C) has that exact structure. (A) and (B) are affirming-the-consequent fallacies. (D) is a disjunctive syllogism. (E) is categorical (universal → instance). Match shape, not subject matter.
Question 6 · Reading Comprehension · Main Point

Passage (excerpt). Kris writes: "For nearly twenty years our brand has rested on the premise that proximity sells. Critics now argue that fatigue is setting in. They are not wrong about the fatigue, but they have misdiagnosed its cause. Audiences have not tired of proximity; they have tired of manufactured proximity — the heavily produced confessional, the scripted reconciliation. I therefore propose that we reduce our commitment to scripted long-form programming by half and reinvest the freed time into platforms that reward unmediated presence."

Which of the following most accurately expresses the main point of the passage?

  1. Audiences are increasingly fatigued by the family's media output.
  2. The family should shift away from scripted long-form programming and toward platforms that reward unmediated presence, because the fatigue critics identify is with produced content rather than with proximity itself.
  3. Critics of the family's strategy have offered no useful insight.
  4. The family should leave reality television entirely.
  5. Scripted long-form programming has become unprofitable.
Answer: B. The main point must cover the whole passage and reflect the author's conclusion. (A) is a premise. (C) misstates the author's view — she concedes critics are partly right. (D) overreaches; she's recommitting to camera, just differently. (E) imports a claim the passage doesn't make. Only (B) ties the diagnosis to the prescription.
Question 7 · Argumentative Writing · Structure

You are writing an LSAT Argumentative Writing essay and have drafted paragraphs claiming the family should film a wedding episode. You are about to write paragraph three.

Which of the following best describes what paragraph three should do?

  1. Introduce a brand-new reason in favor of filming the wedding.
  2. Restate the thesis using stronger language.
  3. Present the strongest version of the opposing position, concede what is true about it, and explain why it does not change the conclusion.
  4. Provide a personal anecdote about weddings the writer has attended.
  5. Quote the evidence verbatim without commentary.
Answer: C. The counterposition paragraph is what separates a top-rated essay from a middling one. State the opposition fairly, concede what's true, and explain why your position still wins. (A) belongs earlier, (B) belongs in the closer, (D) is what the scorers explicitly downgrade, and (E) misuses the evidence — you must explain its significance, not just quote it.
